# Drinking-water safety and storage

## Do not rely on appearance

Water can look and smell normal while containing bacteria, viruses, parasites or hazardous chemicals. Measurements such as total dissolved solids (TDS), electrical conductivity and turbidity do **not** prove that water is safe to drink.

## Assess the source

Prefer commercially packaged potable water or a supply confirmed safe by the relevant water authority. Follow local boil-water and do-not-drink notices; the required response can differ depending on the contaminant. Boiling kills many infectious organisms but **does not remove toxic chemicals** and may concentrate some of them.

Use an appropriately certified filter and a suitable disinfectant only within the manufacturer's limits. Read the instructions for the actual device and disinfectant, including contact time, filter capacity and contaminants it cannot remove. Avoid assuming that an improvised filter creates safe drinking water.

## Inventory

Record volume stored, container condition, the date of filling or purchase, and any relevant use-by recommendations. Protect drinking-water containers from contamination, heat and sunlight according to supplier guidance. Keep non-potable water clearly labelled and physically separate.

## If uncertain

Contact your water authority or public health authority when possible. For a life-threatening incident, seek emergency help. This note is a reminder, not a water potability certificate.
