# First actions during a disruption

## Prioritise people, not electronics

If there is immediate danger, evacuate or move to a safer location. In Australia dial **000** if telephone service is available. Local emergency broadcasts, official warnings, certified detectors and qualified medical advice take priority over this offline reference. A computer cannot determine whether a building or environment is safe.

## First 10 minutes

1. Check for injury, breathing difficulties, fire, smoke, hazardous fumes, structural hazards and unsafe electrical equipment. Avoid touching damaged electrical conductors.
2. Confirm the location of everybody in your group without entering unsafe areas.
3. If the area is safe, identify reliable drinking water, required medication, torch, battery-powered radio and communications equipment.
4. Keep a written log of times, observations, important instructions and resource use. Note the source and time of any warnings.
5. Decide whether sheltering or leaving is safer using current official advice. Keep multiple evacuation routes available.

## Communications plan

Have an agreed check-in interval, one local and one out-of-area contact, meeting points and a paper copy of critical phone numbers. A mesh radio is useful for nearby communications but does not guarantee contact with emergency services. Test it *before* relying on it.

## Resilience principle

Maintain low-tech alternatives: paper map, printed inventory and contacts, standalone torch, certified alarms, weather radio and independent water treatment supplies. Batteries and digital systems fail.
